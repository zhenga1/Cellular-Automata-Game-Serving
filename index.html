Simple rules, random outcomes

A small world, made of simple rules

Every block in this world follows a handful of local rules. A grain of sand looks at the cell below it and, if it can, falls; water looks sideways too; fire looks at its neighbours and decides, by a roll of the dice, whether to spread. Nothing is scripted and no block knows the bigger picture. The dunes, rivers and wildfires you see are what those tiny rules add up to.

That is the idea of cellular automata: a deliberately simplistic interpretation of the real world. Real sand, water and fire are vastly more complicated, but they share the same character. Their behaviour is random and stochastic: you can never say exactly which grain will tumble left or which leaf will catch next, only what tends to happen. Run the same scene twice and it plays out differently, the way the real world does.

Where this is going

A world for people and AI to build, break and solve together

The next step is a community of people who like building worlds and levels: realistic, human-aligned scenarios built on cellular automata. A village that needs water, a fire that has to be stopped, a flood that has to go somewhere.

Alongside them, AI agents will learn to do all three jobs. They will generate voxel scenes, generate levels and scenarios, and find solutions to them, measured against how people solve the same problems. The aim is agents that get a feel for human situations, for the randomness built into every scene, and for what a good, sensible solution looks like. That would make them better at solving real problems the way people would.

  1. People build

    Worlds and levels with realistic stakes: villages, rivers, fires.

  2. Agents generate

    New voxel scenes and scenarios in the same spirit.

  3. Agents solve

    Levels under the same rules, randomness and limited kit as a player.

  4. People judge

    Which solutions feel right, and which scenes ring true.

and round again, each pass a little closer to how people think
